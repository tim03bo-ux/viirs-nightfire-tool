# Field Guide — Retrieving the 1957 Kimbell/Kincaid Oil-Test File in Person

**For the person going to RRC Central Records.** This is a self-contained packet —
you should not need anything except this sheet and the two printed exhibits named in
§3. Read §4 once so the filing system makes sense; work §1, §5, and §6 at the counter.

---

## 0. The mission, in one sentence

Find the Railroad Commission's original paper/microfilm file — the **W‑1 drilling
permit** and **W‑3 plugging record**, plus any driller's/electric log, plat, or
correspondence — for a **shallow oil test drilled May 14, 1957** on the **Kimbell
lease, Section 3, Abstract A‑159, G.C.&S.F. Ry. Co. Survey, Upton County (District
7C)**, that came up dry, was **plugged back, and converted to a water well**.

It is **not** online. Every digitized RRC collection has been searched (see §8). The
file, if it survives, is in the **pre‑1965 physical records** filed by operator number.

## 4. How the pre‑1965 filing system works (read this once)

Before 1965 the RRC had **no API numbers and no lease numbers.** To organize records,
**each operator was assigned a 5‑digit "operator number,"** and **all of that
operator's records were filed under that number, then alphabetically by lease name.**

So the retrieval path is:

> **Operator name → operator's 5‑digit number → the lease file (alphabetical)**

That means the two things that actually find the file are the **operator name**
(Kimbell / Kimbrell / Kimble …) and the **lease name** (Kimbell, or Kincaid) — **not**
a well or API number, which don't exist for this era. The counter staff know this
system; your job is to give them the operator and lease names and the location.

## 5. At the counter — retrieval procedure

**Say this:** *"I'm looking for a pre‑1965 oil‑and‑gas well file, District 7C, Upton
County, Section 3 of the G.C.&S.F. survey, Abstract A‑159. Operator is Kimbell (also
spelled Kimbrell / Kimble) Oil Company; the lease is the Kimbell lease; the surface
owner was Bert Kincaid. The well was drilled May 14, 1957, came up dry, and was
plugged back. I need the W‑1 permit and the W‑3 plugging record."*

1. **Operator number.** Ask staff to look up the **5‑digit operator number** for
   **Kimbell / Kimbrell / Kimble / Kimball Oil Company**. Write down every number they
   find — the outfit may appear under more than one spelling.
2. **Pull the lease file.** Under that operator number, ask for the lease filed as
   **Kimbell** (and/or **Kincaid**). Section 3 / A‑159 confirms you have the right one.
3. **Read the file.** Look for and photograph/copy **each** of:
   - **W‑1** — Application/Permit to Drill (the original 1957 permit)
   - **W‑3** — Plugging Record (depths, plugs, plug‑back)
   - any **driller's log or electric log**, and the **location plat**
   - any **letters/correspondence** (dry‑hole notices often live here)
4. **Confirm it's the right well** before you leave — see §7.

## 6. If the primary search fails — fallback tree

Work these in order; each is a different way in.

- **Fallback 1 — by location.** Ask whether there's a **county / abstract cross‑index**
  for pre‑1965 wells, and search **Sec 3, A‑159, G.C.&S.F., Upton**. Location can find
  a file when the operator name is misfiled.
- **Fallback 2 — the ARCO cross‑reference.** ARCO drilled **Kimbell #1** (API
  **42‑461‑32677**) on **this same section** in 1987. Ask to see the **lease jacket**
  or **well file for "Kimbell, Section 3"** — a lease jacket often accumulates *older*
  wellbore records on the same tract, and the 1987 file may point back to the 1957
  hole. (You already hold the imaged 1987 file — exhibit 3.)
- **Fallback 3 — the people.** The water‑well driller was **"Buck Jones, McCamey."**
  The oil‑test *operator* may be different from him. Push on operator **Kimbrell** and
  on any small McCamey/Rankin‑area operator of the late 1950s.
- **Fallback 4 — the District 7C office.** The **San Angelo district office** keeps
  district well records (the RRC "District Office Well Records" set). It may hold a
  copy the Austin file doesn't. **622 South Oakes St., Suite J, San Angelo, TX 76903**
  · **san_angelo@rrc.texas.gov** (call the main line for the office's direct number and
  hours before going).
- **Fallback 5 — county land records (off‑site, but decisive).** The **Upton County
  Clerk** in **Rankin** holds deed/lease records: an **oil & gas lease** on the Kincaid
  tract from ~1956–1957 will **name the operator and date** the venture, which then
  unlocks the RRC operator number. The **Upton CAD** can confirm the tract and owner
  history. This is the surest way to nail the operator's exact name if the RRC file is
  filed oddly.

## 7. Confirm you have the *right* well before you leave

The Kimbell/Kincaid name appears on **other, later, deeper wells**. Verify the file in
your hand matches **all** of these, or you may have grabbed the wrong one:

- [ ] **Section 3, A‑159, G.C.&S.F.**, Upton (not "TC RR Sec 3," which is a *different*
      Kincaid well)
- [ ] drilled **~1957** (not the 1980s ARCO well, not a 1960s deep test)
- [ ] **shallow / plugged back** — a few hundred to a couple thousand feet, **not** a
      ~10,000‑ft Bend/Devonian test
- [ ] operator **Kimbell/Kimbrell** and/or owner **Kincaid**

## 8. Why this trip is necessary (for staff who ask)

Every online RRC imaged‑records collection has already been searched and **none** holds
this well: Oil & Gas Well Records (1964→), **Dry Hole Files** (2000→), District Office
Well Records, Well Logs, the full **Wildcat & Suspense** roll WS7C‑2 (only 1965–68),
W‑1X/W‑3X/W‑3C (1988→), and 1963‑&‑Prior Closed Potential (producing fields only). The
well has no API number. A **1957** filing predates all of it — which is why the
**pre‑1965 physical operator file** is the only remaining source.

## 10. If it genuinely isn't there

Some pre‑1965 incomplete records did not survive. If the operator file turns up nothing,
that is itself an answer: the well's existence is already fully documented by the 1966
USGS/TWDB card, the RRC GIS location, and the ARCO 1987 lease/section records in this
packet — and the **Upton County Clerk lease records** (§6, Fallback 5) remain the best
independent route to the operator's identity.
